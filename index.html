Formulas

輸入採報告單位：Na/K/Cl mEq/L；Lactate/Ca/Mg/Phosphate mg/dL；Albumin g/dL
HCO₃ = 0.0301 × PaCO₂ × 10^(pH − 6.1)
Lactate(mmol/L)=Lactate(mg/dL)×0.111；Ca(mmol/L)=Ca(mg/dL)×0.25；Mg(mmol/L)=Mg(mEq/L)÷2；Phosphate(mmol/L)=Phosphate(mg/dL)×0.323
SIDa = Na + K + 2×Ca(mmol/L) + 2×Mg(mmol/L) − Cl − Lactate(mmol/L)
Albumin charge = Albumin(g/L) × (0.123×pH − 0.631)
Phosphate charge = Phosphate(mmol/L) × (0.309×pH − 0.469)
SIDe = HCO₃ + Albumin charge + Phosphate charge
SIG = SIDa − SIDe

參考範圍採實用臨床 rule of thumb：SIDa 約 38–42 mEq/L、SIG 約 0–5 mEq/L。

Ca 若可取得 ionized calcium，理論上較適合 Stewart calculation；本版暫以 Ca mg/dL 轉換估算。

Physiologic meaning & clinical interpretation

SIDa (apparent strong ion difference)
Represents the measured difference between strong cations and strong anions. Primarily reflects chloride/lactate-related metabolic effects.
Low SIDa usually indicates chloride- or lactate-driven metabolic acidosis.
High SIDa usually indicates hypochloremic or contraction alkalosis.

SIDe (effective strong ion difference)
Represents the effective charge balance explained by bicarbonate and weak acids (albumin and phosphate).
Affected mainly by bicarbonate concentration, albumin, and phosphate.

SIG (strong ion gap)
Represents unmeasured ions not explained by routine electrolytes.
Elevated SIG suggests excess unmeasured anions such as:

  • uremia
  • ketoacidosis
  • toxic alcohols
  • salicylate
  • sepsis-related anions
Albumin effect
Low albumin reduces weak acids and produces an alkalinizing effect, which may mask metabolic acidosis.
